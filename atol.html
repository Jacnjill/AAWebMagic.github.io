ATOL protection for holidays with flights

Last updated: 30 October 2026

Trip N Travel acts as agent. ATOL protection comes from the ATOL holder named on your ATOL Certificate.

We do not hold an ATOL of our own. When we book a package holiday that includes a flight, the holiday company that supplies the package provides the ATOL protection. That holiday company is the ATOL holder.

Not every service is ATOL protected, so please check your ATOL Certificate to see what it covers.

What ATOL means

In the words of the UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA), which runs the scheme:

“ATOL (which stands for Air Travel Organisers’ Licensing) is financial protection when you book a package trip that includes a flight. It is not travel insurance.”

“ATOL holders can sell flights and packages through travel agents and therefore travel agents do not need to hold their own ATOL. On making an ATOL protected booking you will be issued with documentation, including an ATOL Certificate, stating the name of the ATOL holder who is providing your protection.”

The CAA says that if your travel company becomes a failed ATOL holder while you are abroad, it will help you continue your trip by:

  • assisting you to stay in your holiday accommodation, if possible
  • reimbursing you for money you may have spent to replace ATOL protected parts of your trip
  • arranging flights home, depending on the circumstances.

ATOL helps when an ATOL holder fails. The CAA says you cannot claim from ATOL for a cancelled or delayed flight, or for cancellations, complaints, sickness or injury when the holiday company is still trading. ATOL is not travel insurance, and the CAA advises you to buy travel insurance.

Source: UK Civil Aviation Authority, What is ATOL protection?

Your ATOL Certificate

When you book a package holiday with flights through us, the holiday company issues your ATOL Certificate. Look at the section “Who is protecting your trip?” to see which ATOL holder protects your booking. Keep your certificate with your travel documents.
